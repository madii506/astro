01 / Start

What Astro is

Astro is a launchpad with its own chain. Coins launch on a bonding curve, like on pump.fun, but every transaction has to get through a gate before it can reach a block. The gate checks five rules: nobody but the creator buys in the launch block, every wallet gets one buy of at most 1% in the first minute, wallets that share a funder can't all buy, the creator's buy is capped at 3%, and the creator's tokens are locked for a day.

The point isn't to pick winners. It's to make the start of every launch fair, so that the first minute belongs to people instead of bots, bundles and the dev.

!

The most important thing first: the Astro chain is not live yet. What runs today is the gate's logic applied to real Solana data: a scan for any Solana launch. The chain goes to a public testnet before it goes anywhere else.

03 / The gate

Why a launchpad needs its own chain

On Solana a launchpad is a program. It runs when a transaction calls it, and by then the important decisions have already been made somewhere else:

  • The block producer decides what shares the launch block. A sniper can bundle a buy right behind the create transaction and pay a tip to have it included. The launchpad's program sees a valid buy and executes it.
  • A program can't see where a wallet's money came from. Twenty wallets funded by one person look like twenty strangers.
  • Rules on a website are promises. Anyone can skip a website and call the program directly.

Astro moves the rules in front of the block producer. Every transaction is checked by the gate first, against the chain's own state, and a transaction that breaks a rule is never included. That's the only place these rules can be enforced rather than promised.

04 / The gate

The five rules

Every coin on Astro launches under the same five rules. There are no settings: a creator can't switch them off and nobody can pay to skip them.

Rule 1 · The launch block is for the launch

A coin is created in a block of its own. That block holds the create transaction and the creator's own first buy, and nothing else. Any other buy that targets the launch block is refused. Trading opens in the next block, for everyone at once.

Rule 2 · One buy each, 1% max, first minute

For the first 60 seconds after launch, each wallet gets exactly one buy, and that buy can take at most 1% of the supply. A second buy inside the minute is refused. A buy for more than 1% is trimmed to 1%. Sells are always allowed.

Rule 3 · No common funder, first hour

During a coin's first hour, the gate looks at the last wallet that sent SOL to a buyer in the previous 24 hours. If that funder already funded another buyer of the same coin, the buy is refused. The first wallet from any funder gets in; the rest don't. Known exchange hot wallets are exempt, because thousands of unrelated people withdraw from them.

Rule 4 · The dev buy is capped at 3%

The creator's buy in the launch block can take at most 3% of supply. For the first hour, the creator's wallet and any wallet the creator funded in the previous 24 hours can't buy again.

Rule 5 · The dev is locked for 24 hours

The creator's tokens can't be sold or transferred for 24 hours after launch. The lock is on the tokens, not the wallet, so sending them to a fresh wallet first doesn't get around it.

i

Rules only change in the open. Any change is published with its reasoning before it takes effect, and coins that launched under the old rules keep them.

06 / Tools

The gate scan

The gate scan takes any Solana token and replays its launch through the five rules, using only public data read straight from Solana. It isn't tied to one launchpad: pump.fun coins, bonk and Meteora launches, Moonshot coins and tokens that opened a Raydium or Orca pool all work. You don't need a wallet. Here is exactly what it does:

  1. Reads the coin. It reads the mint and its name. If it's a pump.fun coin, it also reads the creator recorded on its bonding curve; for anything else, the creator is the wallet that made the mint.
  2. Finds when trading opened. It walks the coin's history back to the start and looks for the first time tokens landed in an account owned by a program: a bonding curve or a pool. That block is the launch block. On a launchpad it's the creation itself; for a token minted first and pooled later, it's the pool opening, and anyone who already held tokens by then is a pre-launch holder.
  3. Reads the first minute. It loads every transaction in the launch block and in the 60 seconds after it (up to 140 of them) and works out which wallets gained or lost tokens in each one. Pools and curves are recognised because their accounts belong to programs, not people, so they never count as buyers.
  4. Traces the money. For up to 22 of the earliest buyers it looks one step back: who sent each wallet SOL just before it bought, and who signed the buy. Wallets sharing a funder, or bought for by the same signer, are grouped.
  5. Reads the dev's first day. It reads the creator's token account for the 24 hours after launch and records every time tokens left it.
  6. Replays it through the gate. Every first-minute event is run through the rules in order: launch block first, then shared funders, then second buys, then the 1% cap. Each buy is passed, refused or trimmed.

Results are cached for two minutes for young coins and an hour for coins older than a day, so a popular coin doesn't get read from scratch every time.

08 / Tools

What the tools can't see

  • Funding more than one hop back. The scan follows the money one step. A funder who routes through a middle wallet first won't be grouped.
  • Very busy coins. Reaching the launch means walking back through a coin's whole history. On the public RPC that stops at about 30,000 transactions; with a faster RPC, about 120,000.
  • Other wallets of the dev. The dev check reads one creator wallet, not other wallets the creator might control.
  • Wallets that are programs. A buyer whose wallet is itself a program account, like some multisig vaults, is treated as a pool and left out.
  • Pools that opened much later. For a token minted first and pooled later, the scan looks for the pool opening in the first 12 transactions of its history.

09 / The chain

How the chain runs

Astro is built in three layers:

  • The gate receives every transaction first. It checks the five rules against the chain's current state, then forwards the transaction or refuses it. A refused transaction is never included and costs nothing.
  • The sequencer orders the transactions the gate let through and produces a block every 400 milliseconds. At launch there is a single sequencer, run by the team, as with most new chains.
  • The blocks are public. Anyone can run a read-only node, replay every block and check that every included transaction was allowed by the rules.

More block producers come in a later stage. Until then, the honest description is: one operator, with every action public and checkable.

10 / The chain

The bridge and $ASTRO

$ASTRO is a token on Solana. On the Astro chain it's the native unit: launches are paid in it and bonding curves are priced in it. It moves between the two one-for-one through a vault on Solana. Deposits into the vault are minted on Astro; burns on Astro are paid out of the vault. Every deposit and withdrawal is public on both sides.

!

At launch the vault is operated by the team. That's a trust assumption, and we'd rather say it plainly than hide it. The vault's balance and every movement are public so anyone can check it matches the supply on Astro.

11 / The chain

Fees

Launches and trades on Astro pay fees in $ASTRO. The exact numbers are published before the testnet, together with where every fee goes. Until then, anything you see quoted elsewhere isn't from us.

The tools on this site are free and need no wallet.

Risks

      
    
        
  • A fair start isn't a safe coin. Most coins go to zero, fair or not.
    • 
        
  • The chain isn't live. Plans can change, and nothing on this page is a promise of a date.
    • 
        
  • One operator at launch. The sequencer and the bridge vault start under the team's control. Everything they do is public, but you're trusting them to keep doing it.
    • 
        
  • Heuristics have edges. The funder check follows one hop and exempts exchanges; determined people can work around parts of it. The rules make cheating slower and more expensive, not impossible.
