T−7The trench

Most launches are decided before you can click.

A coin goes live, and in that same block bots have already bought. Twenty wallets funded by one person take a third of the supply. The dev sells into your first buy. By the time a human sees the chart, the launch is over, and the people who came early were never people.

Snipers

Bots watch for new coins and buy in the launch block itself, often bundled right next to the create transaction. Nobody beats a buy that lands before the chart exists.

Bundles

One person funds twenty fresh wallets and buys with all of them at once. On the chart it looks like twenty believers. It's one hand on a third of the supply.

Dev dumps

The creator buys big at the lowest price, waits for the first wave, then sells into it. Nothing on a normal launchpad can stop them.

T−6The gate

01 / 06

Block zero belongs to the launch.

On Astro, a coin is born in a block of its own. The creator's first buy goes in with it, capped at 3% of the supply. Nothing else fits in that block: not a sniper, not a bundle, not a friend of the dev.

RULE 1 · no buys in the launch block
02 / 06

Snipers bounce.

Bots that try to land in the launch block don't get a worse price. They get refused. The gate checks the block before it checks anything else, so being fastest stops being an edge.

RULE 1 · refused, not front-run
03 / 06

One funder, one wallet.

Before a buy gets in, the gate looks at where the wallet's SOL came from. Eight wallets fed by the same address are one buyer in eight masks. The first one gets in. The other seven don't.

RULE 3 · no common funder, first hour
04 / 06

One buy each. 1% max.

For the first sixty seconds every wallet gets exactly one buy, capped at 1% of the supply. A second buy is refused. A whale's buy is trimmed to the cap. The first minute gets shared, not swallowed.

RULE 2 · one buy per wallet, 1% cap
05 / 06

The dev waits a day.

The creator's tokens can't be sold or moved for 24 hours. Not to another wallet, not to an exchange. If the dev wants out, they leave with everyone else, not before.

RULE 5 · 24-hour dev lock
06 / 06

Then the gate opens.

After the first minute the coin trades freely, for everyone, at the same time. Same block, same price, same rules. That's what a fair start looks like.

T−5Five rules

Five rules. No exceptions. No settings.

Every coin on Astro launches under the same rules. A creator can't switch them off, and nobody can pay to skip them. They're checked for every transaction before it reaches a block.

1

The launch block is for the launch.

A new coin's first block holds the creation and the creator's capped buy. Every other buy aimed at that block is refused.

stops snipersstops create bundles
2

One buy each, 1% max, first minute.

For sixty seconds after launch each wallet gets one buy, capped at 1% of supply. A second buy is refused; a bigger one is trimmed to the cap.

stops whalesstops spam buys
3

No common funder, first hour.

If the wallet that funded you in the last day already funded another buyer of this coin, your buy is refused. Exchange withdrawals don't count.

stops bundlesstops wallet farms
4

The dev buy is capped at 3%.

The creator can buy up to 3% of supply at launch, and that's it for the first hour: no top-ups from the dev's wallet or from wallets it funded.

stops dev-heavy launches
5

The dev is locked for 24 hours.

The creator's tokens can't be sold or transferred for a full day after launch. Moving them to a fresh wallet doesn't work either: the lock follows the tokens.

stops dev dumpsstops slow rugs

None of this makes a coin a good investment. It makes the start fair: everyone who shows up in the first minute plays by the same rules as everyone else.

T−4Its own chain

Rules a launchpad can't enforce, a chain can.

On Solana, a launchpad is a program. It can't choose which transactions share a block with the launch, it can't see where a wallet's money came from, and it can't stop a bundle that pays to go first. Astro is a chain built for launches. The gate sits in front of the block producer, so every rule is checked before a transaction is ever included.

01

The gate

Every transaction meets the gate first. It checks the five rules against the chain's own state, then lets it through or refuses it. A refused transaction never reaches a block and costs nothing.

02

The sequencer

Astro starts with a single block producer, like most new chains, making a block every 400 milliseconds. Every block is public, and anyone can run a read-only node to check the rules were kept.

03

The bridge

$ASTRO moves between Solana and Astro one-for-one through a public vault. On Astro it's the chain's own unit: launches are paid in it and curves are priced in it.

04

The curve

Coins trade on a bonding curve, like on pump.fun. The difference is everything in front of the curve: who's allowed to buy, how much, and when.

T−3Try the gate

T−1Questions

The obvious questions, answered first.

Is Astro live?

Partly, and we'd rather say exactly which part. The gate scan is live today and reads real Solana data. The Astro chain itself isn't live yet: it goes to a public testnet first, then mainnet.

Why does a launchpad need its own chain?

Because the tricks happen before a launchpad's program ever runs. On Solana, whoever pays the block producer the most decides what shares the launch block, and a program can't see who funded a wallet. Astro puts its rules in front of the block producer, where they can actually be enforced.

How does the gate know wallets are bundled?

It follows the money one step back. When a wallet tries to buy, the gate checks which address sent it SOL in the last 24 hours. If that address already funded another buyer of the same coin, the buy is refused. One funder, one wallet.

Can't a sniper just use exchange withdrawals?

They can, which is why exchange hot wallets don't count as a shared funder. But it doesn't help much: in the first minute every wallet gets one buy of at most 1%, at the same price as everyone else. Fifty fresh wallets buy fifty small slices, never the launch block.

What does the gate scan actually check?

It finds the block where trading opened, the moment the coin's curve or pool got its tokens, and reads every transaction in the minute after it from Solana. It notes who already held tokens before that, traces where the early wallets got their SOL, and reads the dev's token account for the first 24 hours. Then it replays all of it through the five rules. It works on any Solana token, not only pump.fun coins; the docs list every step and its limits.

My coin shows a refused buy that was mine. Why?

The scan doesn't know who you are, only what happened. A creator's own launch buy is allowed up to 3%. A second wallet buying in the launch block, even if it's yours, is exactly what the gate refuses.

Is a clean launch a safe coin?

No. A clean launch means nobody cheated at the start. The coin can still go to zero. Nothing on this site is financial advice.

Who runs the chain?

At launch, one sequencer run by the team, like most new chains. The rules, the code and every block are public, and anyone can run a read-only node to check that the gate did what it says. More block producers come in a later stage.

Can the rules change?

Only in the open. Any change to a rule is published with its reasoning before it takes effect, and coins launched under the old rules keep them.

Do I need a wallet to use the scanner?

No. The scan only reads public data. Nothing asks you to connect a wallet or sign anything.
